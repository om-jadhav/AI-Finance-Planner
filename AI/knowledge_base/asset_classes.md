# Asset Classes Used in This App

## Stocks (Equity)

A stock represents partial ownership in a company. When you buy a
company's stock, its value rises or falls with the company's performance
and how investors perceive its future prospects. Individual stocks tend
to be more volatile than diversified funds, since a single company's
fortunes can change quickly. This app selects individual stocks based on
their historical CAGR, volatility, maximum drawdown, and Sharpe ratio,
calculated from real historical price data — never invented.

## Mutual Funds

A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it in a basket
of stocks, bonds, or other securities, managed by a professional fund
manager. Because a mutual fund already holds many underlying securities,
it is inherently more diversified than a single stock.

## ETFs (Exchange-Traded Funds)

An ETF is a fund that trades on the stock exchange like a regular stock,
but tracks the performance of an underlying asset or index. Examples
include ETFs that track a broad equity index, a banking-sector index, or
the price of gold or silver — letting you invest in that underlying asset
without holding it directly. ETFs are popular for SIP (Systematic
Investment Plan) investing — putting in a fixed amount every month —
because they are typically low-cost and diversified. In this app's data,
ETF-type instruments are grouped together with index-tracking funds under
the "etf_instruments" and "index_funds" categories.

## Gold

Gold has historically been used as a store of value, particularly during
periods of economic uncertainty or high inflation, since it doesn't rely
on any single company or government to hold its value. It is often
included in a portfolio as a stabilizing asset rather than the primary
growth driver, since its long-term average returns are typically lower
than equity, but it can behave differently from stocks during market
downturns. In this app, gold-tracking instruments are grouped inside the
"etf_instruments" category.

## Fixed Income (e.g., PPF, Fixed Deposits)

Fixed-income instruments offer a predetermined or predictable rate of
return with very low volatility. They are generally used for the "stable
core" of a portfolio — money that shouldn't be exposed to market swings.
In this app, these appear under the "fixed_income" category.

## Index Funds

An index fund tracks a broad market index (rather than one company),
giving diversified exposure to "the overall market" in a single
instrument. In this app, this appears under the "index_funds" category
and is often used as a benchmark for comparison.
